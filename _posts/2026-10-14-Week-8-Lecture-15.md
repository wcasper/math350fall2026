## Practice problems

Throughout, $$(M,d_M)$$ and $$(N,d_N)$$ denote metric spaces.

1. **Choosing delta for a quadratic function.** Consider

   $$
   f(x)=x^2.
   $$

   We will prove directly that

   $$
   \lim_{x\to 3}x^2=9.
   $$

   1. Factor $$\lvert x^2-9\rvert$$ in a way that separates the factor $$\lvert x-3\rvert$$.
   2. Assume that $$\lvert x-3\rvert<1$$. Prove that

      $$
      \lvert x+3\rvert<7.
      $$

   3. Given $$\varepsilon>0$$, choose

      $$
      \delta=\min\left\{1,\frac{\varepsilon}{7}\right\}.
      $$

      Prove that

      $$
      0<\lvert x-3\rvert<\delta
      \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
      \lvert x^2-9\rvert<\varepsilon.
      $$

   4. Explain the two different jobs performed by the numbers $$1$$ and $$\varepsilon/7$$ in the definition of $$\delta$$.
   5. Find a value of $$\delta$$ that works when $$\varepsilon=0.1$$.

2. **A reciprocal limit.** Prove directly from the epsilon-delta definition that

   $$
   \lim_{x\to 2}\frac1x=\frac12.
   $$

   1. Show that

      $$
      \left\lvert\frac1x-\frac12\right\rvert
      =\frac{\lvert x-2\rvert}{2\lvert x\rvert}.
      $$

   2. Explain why the restriction $$\lvert x-2\rvert<1$$ guarantees that $$\lvert x\rvert>1$$.
   3. Use these observations to find a suitable formula for $$\delta$$ in terms of $$\varepsilon$$.
   4. Write a complete epsilon-delta proof of the limit.
   5. Why is it important to control the denominator before trying to make the numerator small?

3. **Using sequences to test a two-variable limit.** Define

   $$
   F(x,y)=\frac{xy}{x^2+y^2}
   $$

   for $$(x,y)\ne(0,0)$$.

   1. Let

      $$
      p_n=\left(\frac1n,\frac1n\right)
      \qquad\text{and}\qquad
      q_n=\left(\frac1n,-\frac1n\right).
      $$

      Prove that both sequences converge to $$(0,0)$$ in $$\mathbb{R}^2$$.
   2. Compute $$F(p_n)$$ and $$F(q_n)$$.
   3. Use the sequential criterion for limits to prove that

      $$
      \lim_{(x,y)\to(0,0)}F(x,y)
      $$

      does not exist.
   4. More generally, compute $$F(x,mx)$$ for a fixed real number $$m$$. What does this tell you about approaching the origin along different lines?
   5. Explain why checking only the two coordinate axes would fail to detect the problem.

4. **A continuous function on every metric space.** Fix a point $$p\in M$$ and define

   $$
   f:M\to\mathbb{R},
   \qquad
   f(x)=d_M(x,p).
   $$

   1. Use the triangle inequality to prove the reverse triangle inequality

      $$
      \big\lvert d_M(x,p)-d_M(y,p)\big\rvert
      \leq d_M(x,y).
      $$

   2. Let $$a\in M$$ and $$\varepsilon>0$$. Find a value of $$\delta$$ such that

      $$
      d_M(x,a)<\delta
      \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
      \lvert f(x)-f(a)\rvert<\varepsilon.
      $$

   3. Conclude that the distance-to-$$p$$ function is continuous at every point of $$M$$.
   4. If $$x_n\to a$$ in $$M$$, prove directly that

      $$
      d_M(x_n,p)\longrightarrow d_M(a,p).
      $$

   5. Explain how part 4 also follows from the sequential criterion for continuity.

5. **Continuity at a point with oscillation.** Define

   $$
   g(x)=
   \begin{cases}
   x\sin(1/x),&x\ne 0,\\
   0,&x=0.
   \end{cases}
   $$

   1. Prove that

      $$
      \lvert g(x)\rvert\leq\lvert x\rvert
      $$

      for every $$x\in\mathbb{R}$$.
   2. Use the epsilon-delta definition to prove that $$g$$ is continuous at $$0$$.
   3. Let $$(x_n)$$ be any sequence satisfying $$x_n\to0$$. Use the inequality in part 1 to prove that $$g(x_n)\to0$$.
   4. Explain how part 3 gives a second proof of continuity at $$0$$.
   5. The factor $$\sin(1/x)$$ has no limit as $$x\to0$$. Explain why this does not prevent $$g(x)$$ from having a limit.

6. **Preimages and composition.** Let

   $$
   f:M\to N
   \qquad\text{and}\qquad
   h:N\to\mathbb{R}
   $$

   be continuous functions.

   1. Let $$U\subseteq\mathbb{R}$$ be open. Prove that

      $$
      (h\circ f)^{-1}(U)=f^{-1}\big(h^{-1}(U)\big).
      $$

   2. Use the open-set characterization of continuity to prove that $$h\circ f$$ is continuous.
   3. Prove the same result using the epsilon-delta definition of continuity.
   4. Let $$C\subseteq\mathbb{R}$$ be closed. Explain why $$(h\circ f)^{-1}(C)$$ is closed in $$M$$.
   5. Take $$M=N=\mathbb{R}$$, $$f(x)=x^2$$, and $$h(y)=\sin y$$. Find the preimages

      $$
      (h\circ f)^{-1}((-1,1))
      $$

      and

      $$
      (h\circ f)^{-1}(\{0\}).
      $$

      Verify directly that the first set is open and the second set is closed.
