sequence, convergence, divergence, limit of a sequence, $$\varepsilon$$--$$N$$ definition, eventually, bounded sequence, uniqueness of limits, convergence in a metric space

## Practice problems

Throughout, $$(X,d)$$ denotes a metric space and $$(x_n)_{n=1}^{\infty}$$ denotes a sequence in $$X$$.

1. **Sequences and convergence.** Consider the sequences

   $$
   a_n=\frac{n-1}{n+1},
   \qquad
   b_n=(-1)^n,
   \qquad
   c_n=\frac{(-1)^n}{n}.
   $$

   1. Write the first six terms of each sequence.
   2. Determine which sequences appear to converge and identify their possible limits.
   3. For each convergent sequence, explain what happens to the terms after sufficiently many terms have been removed.
   4. Use the definition of convergence to explain why $$(b_n)$$ does not converge.
   5. Explain why the first one hundred terms of a sequence cannot, by themselves, determine whether the sequence converges.

2. **A direct $$\varepsilon$$--$$N$$ proof.** Let

   $$
   a_n=\frac{3n-1}{n+2}.
   $$

   1. Guess the limit of $$(a_n)$$.
   2. Simplify

      $$
      \left\lvert a_n-3\right\rvert.
      $$

   3. Prove your answer directly from the definition of convergence. Your proof must give an explicit choice of $$N$$ in terms of $$\varepsilon$$.
   4. Find a value of $$N$$ that works when

      $$
      \varepsilon=0.01.
      $$

3. **Reading the quantifiers.** Suppose that $$(x_n)$$ is a sequence in $$X$$ and $$x\in X$$. For each statement below, decide whether it is equivalent to $$x_n\to x$$. Prove your answer or give a counterexample.

   1. For every $$\varepsilon>0$$, there exists $$N\in\mathbb{N}$$ such that

      $$
      d(x_n,x)<\varepsilon
      $$

      for at least one $$n\ge N$$.

   2. There exists $$N\in\mathbb{N}$$ such that, for every $$\varepsilon>0$$ and every $$n\ge N$$,

      $$
      d(x_n,x)<\varepsilon.
      $$

   3. For every $$\varepsilon>0$$, there exists $$N\in\mathbb{N}$$ such that

      $$
      d(x_n,x)\le\varepsilon
      $$

      whenever $$n\ge N$$.

   4. For every $$k\in\mathbb{N}$$, there exists $$N_k\in\mathbb{N}$$ such that

      $$
      d(x_n,x)<\frac1k
      $$

      whenever $$n\ge N_k$$.

4. **Uniqueness and finite changes.**

   1. Suppose that

      $$
      x_n\to x
      \qquad\text{and}\qquad
      x_n\to y.
      $$

      Assuming that $$x\ne y$$, choose an appropriate value of $$\varepsilon$$ involving $$d(x,y)$$ and use the triangle inequality to obtain a contradiction. Conclude that a convergent sequence has a unique limit.

   2. Suppose that $$x_n\to x$$ and that $$(y_n)$$ is another sequence for which there exists $$M\in\mathbb{N}$$ such that

      $$
      y_n=x_n
      $$

      whenever $$n\ge M$$. Prove that $$y_n\to x$$.

   3. Give two sequences whose first one hundred terms are different but which converge to the same point.

   4. Give an example showing that changing infinitely many terms of a sequence can preserve convergence.

   5. Give an example showing that changing infinitely many terms can destroy convergence.

5. **Convergence in metric spaces.**

   1. Suppose that $$x_n\to x$$ in a metric space. Use the definition with $$\varepsilon=1$$ to show that all sufficiently late terms lie in the ball

      $$
      B(x;1).
      $$

   2. Explain why the finitely many earlier terms can be included in one larger ball centered at $$x$$.

   3. Conclude that every convergent sequence is bounded.

   4. Give a bounded sequence in $$\mathbb{R}$$ that does not converge.

   5. Now equip a set $$X$$ with the discrete metric

      $$
      d(x,y)=
      \begin{cases}
      0,&x=y,\\
      1,&x\ne y.
      \end{cases}
      $$

      Prove that a sequence in $$X$$ converges to $$x$$ if and only if it is eventually equal to $$x$$.

6. **Accumulation points and sequences.** Let $$A\subseteq X$$ and suppose that $$x$$ is an accumulation point of $$A$$.

   1. Explain why every ball centered at $$x$$ contains infinitely many points of $$A$$.
   2. For each $$n\in\mathbb{N}$$, choose a point $$x_n\in A$$ so that the points $$x_n$$ are distinct and

      $$
      0<d(x_n,x)<\frac1n.
      $$

   3. Prove directly from the definition that

      $$
      x_n\to x.
      $$

   4. Conversely, suppose that there is a sequence of distinct points $$x_n\in A$$ such that $$x_n\to x$$. Prove that $$x$$ is an accumulation point of $$A$$.

   5. State the resulting characterization of accumulation points using convergent sequences.
