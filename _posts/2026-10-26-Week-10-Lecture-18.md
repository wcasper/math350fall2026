## Practice problems

Throughout, metric subspaces carry the metric inherited from the ambient space.

1. **Building continuous functions algebraically.** Define

   $$
   F(x,y)=\frac{x^2+3y}{1+x^2+y^2}.
   $$

   1. Explain why the coordinate functions

      $$
      \pi_1(x,y)=x
      \qquad\text{and}\qquad
      \pi_2(x,y)=y
      $$

      are continuous from $$\mathbb{R}^2$$ to $$\mathbb{R}$$.

   2. Use the algebra of continuous functions to prove that the numerator and denominator of $$F$$ are continuous.

   3. Prove that

      $$
      1+x^2+y^2>0
      $$

      for every $$(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^2$$, and conclude that $$F$$ is continuous on all of $$\mathbb{R}^2$$.

   4. Let

      $$
      G(x,y)=\frac{x+y}{x^2+y^2-1}.
      $$

      Determine the largest subset of $$\mathbb{R}^2$$ on which this formula defines a continuous function.

   5. Is the domain found in part 4 connected? Justify your answer.

2. **Distance from a compact set.** Let $$(S,d)$$ be a metric space, let $$A\subseteq S$$ be a nonempty compact set, and fix $$p\in S$$. Define

   $$
   f:A\to\mathbb{R},
   \qquad
   f(x)=d(x,p).
   $$

   1. Use the reverse triangle inequality to prove that

      $$
      \lvert f(x)-f(y)\rvert\leq d(x,y)
      $$

      for all $$x,y\in A$$.

   2. Conclude that $$f$$ is continuous on $$A$$.

   3. Use the extreme value theorem to prove that there exist $$a,b\in A$$ such that

      $$
      d(a,p)\leq d(x,p)\leq d(b,p)
      $$

      for every $$x\in A$$.

   4. Interpret $$a$$ and $$b$$ geometrically.

   5. Suppose that $$p\notin A$$. Prove that

      $$
      d(a,p)>0.
      $$

      Thus a point outside a compact set has positive distance from that set.

   6. Explain what can fail without compactness by considering both of the following examples:

      - $$A=(0,1)$$ and $$p=0$$, where the infimum distance is not attained;
      - $$A=\mathbb{R}$$ and $$p=0$$, where the distance function has no maximum.

3. **Connected spaces cannot be continuously split into labels.** Let $$S$$ be a nonempty connected metric space.

   1. Suppose that $$f:S\to\{0,1\}$$ is continuous, where $$\{0,1\}$$ has the metric inherited from $$\mathbb{R}$$. Prove that $$f(S)$$ is connected.

   2. Prove that the only nonempty connected subsets of $$\{0,1\}$$ are $$\{0\}$$ and $$\{1\}$$.

   3. Conclude that every continuous function

      $$
      f:S\to\{0,1\}
      $$

      is constant.

   4. More generally, prove that every continuous function

      $$
      f:S\to\mathbb{Z}
      $$

      is constant.

   5. Let $$A\subseteq S$$ be both open and closed in $$S$$. Define

      $$
      \chi_A(x)=
      \begin{cases}
      1,&x\in A,\\
      0,&x\notin A.
      \end{cases}
      $$

      Prove that $$\chi_A:S\to\{0,1\}$$ is continuous.

   6. Use the preceding parts to prove that the only subsets of a connected space that are both open and closed are $$\varnothing$$ and $$S$$.

4. **Connected subsets of the real line have no gaps.** Let $$A\subseteq\mathbb{R}$$ be connected, and suppose that $$x,z\in A$$ with $$x<z$$.

   1. Let $$y$$ satisfy $$x<y<z$$. Suppose, toward a contradiction, that $$y\notin A$$.

   2. Define

      $$
      U=A\cap(-\infty,y)
      \qquad\text{and}\qquad
      V=A\cap(y,\infty).
      $$

      Prove that $$U$$ and $$V$$ are open in the subspace $$A$$.

   3. Prove that $$U$$ and $$V$$ are nonempty, disjoint, and satisfy

      $$
      A=U\cup V.
      $$

   4. Explain why this contradicts the connectedness of $$A$$.

   5. Conclude that whenever $$x,z\in A$$ and $$x<z$$, every point between $$x$$ and $$z$$ also belongs to $$A$$.

   6. Combine this result with the theorem that every interval is connected to prove:

      $$
      A\subseteq\mathbb{R}\text{ is connected}
      \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
      A\text{ is an interval},
      $$

      where singletons and the empty set are allowed as degenerate intervals.

5. **Applications of the intermediate value theorem.** Let $$f,g:[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}$$ be continuous.

   1. Suppose that

      $$
      f(a)<g(a)
      \qquad\text{and}\qquad
      f(b)>g(b).
      $$

      Define $$h=f-g$$ and prove that there exists $$c\in(a,b)$$ such that

      $$
      f(c)=g(c).
      $$

   2. Use part 1 to prove that every continuous function

      $$
      f:[0,1]\to[0,1]
      $$

      has a point $$c\in[0,1]$$ satisfying

      $$
      f(c)=1-c.
      $$

   3. Prove that the equation

      $$
      x^5+x=3
      $$

      has a solution in $$(1,2)$$.

   4. If $$y>x$$, prove that

      $$
      y^5+y>x^5+x.
      $$

      Conclude that the solution in part 3 is unique.

   5. Explain each of the preceding applications in terms of the fact that the continuous image of an interval is connected.

6. **Uniform continuity and compactness.** Let $$(S,d_S)$$ and $$(T,d_T)$$ be metric spaces, let $$A\subseteq S$$ be compact, and suppose that $$f:A\to T$$ is continuous.

   1. Suppose that $$f$$ is not uniformly continuous. Prove that there exists $$\varepsilon_0>0$$ and sequences $$(x_n)$$ and $$(y_n)$$ in $$A$$ such that

      $$
      d_S(x_n,y_n)<\frac1n
      $$

      but

      $$
      d_T(f(x_n),f(y_n))\geq\varepsilon_0
      $$

      for every $$n$$.

   2. Use compactness to choose a convergent subsequence

      $$
      x_{n_k}\to p\in A.
      $$

      Prove that $$y_{n_k}\to p$$ as well.

   3. Use continuity of $$f$$ at $$p$$ and the triangle inequality to obtain a contradiction. Conclude that every continuous function on a compact set is uniformly continuous.

   4. Show directly that $$f(x)=x^2$$ is uniformly continuous on every bounded interval. You may use

      $$
      \lvert x^2-y^2\rvert
      =\lvert x-y\rvert\lvert x+y\rvert.
      $$

   5. Let

      $$
      x_n=n
      \qquad\text{and}\qquad
      y_n=n+\frac1n.
      $$

      Prove that $$\lvert x_n-y_n\rvert\to0$$ but

      $$
      \lvert x_n^2-y_n^2\rvert\not\to0.
      $$

      Conclude that $$f(x)=x^2$$ is not uniformly continuous on $$\mathbb{R}$$.

   6. Explain why parts 4 and 5 do not contradict one another.
