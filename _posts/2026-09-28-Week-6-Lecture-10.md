## Sequences and convergence

### Key words

sequence, tail of a sequence, convergence, divergence, limit, $$\varepsilon$$--$$N$$ definition, bounded sequence, uniqueness of limits, algebra of limits, order theorem, squeeze theorem

## Practice problems

Throughout, a sequence in $$\mathbb{R}^m$$ is written $$(x_n)_{n=1}^{\infty}$$. Unless a problem explicitly allows another theorem, prove convergence directly from the definition or from the limit theorems established in lecture.

1. **Reading the definition carefully.** Let

   $$
   a_n=\frac{4n-3}{2n+5}.
   $$

   1. Guess the limit of $$(a_n)$$.
   2. Prove your answer directly from the $$\varepsilon$$--$$N$$ definition. Your proof must give an explicit choice of $$N$$ in terms of $$\varepsilon$$.
   3. Explain why increasing your chosen value of $$N$$ cannot invalidate the proof.

2. **The quantifiers matter.** For each statement, determine whether it is equivalent to $$a_n\to L$$. If it is not equivalent, give a counterexample.

   1. For every $$\varepsilon>0$$, there exists $$N\in\mathbb{N}$$ such that $$\lvert a_n-L\rvert<\varepsilon$$ for at least one $$n\ge N$$.
   2. There exists $$N\in\mathbb{N}$$ such that, for every $$\varepsilon>0$$ and every $$n\ge N$$, $$\lvert a_n-L\rvert<\varepsilon$$.
   3. For every $$\varepsilon>0$$, there exists $$N\in\mathbb{N}$$ such that $$\lvert a_n-L\rvert\le\varepsilon$$ whenever $$n\ge N$$.
   4. For every $$k\in\mathbb{N}$$, there exists $$N_k\in\mathbb{N}$$ such that $$\lvert a_n-L\rvert<1/k$$ whenever $$n\ge N_k$$.

3. **Changing finitely many terms.** Suppose $$a_n\to L$$ and let $$(b_n)$$ be a sequence for which there is some $$M\in\mathbb{N}$$ such that

   $$
   b_n=a_n\qquad\text{for every }n\ge M.
   $$

   1. Prove directly from the definition that $$b_n\to L$$.
   2. Give two sequences that have different first one hundred terms but converge to the same limit.
   3. Can changing infinitely many terms preserve the limit? Give an example and justify your answer.

4. **Uniqueness of the limit.** Suppose that $$a_n\to L$$ and $$a_n\to M$$.

   1. Assuming $$L\ne M$$, choose a useful value of $$\varepsilon$$ involving $$\lvert L-M\rvert$$.
   2. Use the triangle inequality to obtain a contradiction.
   3. Conclude that a convergent sequence has exactly one limit.

5. **Convergent sequences are bounded.** Let $$(x_n)$$ be a sequence in $$\mathbb{R}^m$$ with $$x_n\to x$$.

   1. Use the definition with $$\varepsilon=1$$ to show that the tail of the sequence lies in an open ball centered at $$x$$.
   2. Explain carefully how the finitely many earlier terms can be included in one larger ball.
   3. Conclude that there exists $$R>0$$ such that $$\lVert x_n\rVert\le R$$ for every $$n\in\mathbb{N}$$.
   4. Give a bounded sequence that does not converge.

6. **Limit laws in action.** Suppose $$a_n\to 2$$, $$b_n\to-3$$, and $$c_n\to 5$$. Determine each limit and cite the limit laws you use.

   1. $$3a_n-2b_n+c_n$$.
   2. $$(a_n+b_n)c_n$$.
   3. $$\dfrac{a_n-c_n}{b_n}$$.
   4. $$\dfrac{1}{a_n^2+b_n^2}$$.

   Then explain why the quotient law cannot be applied merely from the information $$d_n\to0$$ to evaluate $$a_n/d_n$$.

7. **An order theorem.** Suppose $$a_n\to a$$ and $$b_n\to b$$, and assume that $$a_n\le b_n$$ for every $$n\in\mathbb{N}$$.

   1. Prove that $$a\le b$$.
   2. Show by example that the strict inequalities $$a_n<b_n$$ for every $$n$$ need not imply $$a<b$$.
   3. Prove the eventual version: if there exists $$N_0$$ such that $$a_n\le b_n$$ whenever $$n\ge N_0$$, then $$a\le b$$.

8. **The squeeze theorem.** Prove that each sequence converges and find its limit.

   1. $$a_n=\dfrac{\sin n}{n}$$.
   2. $$b_n=\dfrac{n+\cos n}{n^2+1}$$.
   3. $$c_n=\sqrt{n^2+3n}-n$$.

   For the third sequence, first rewrite the expression by multiplying by a conjugate and then find suitable upper and lower bounds.

9. **Vector-valued sequences.** Let

   $$
   x_n=\left(\frac{n}{n+1},\frac{(-1)^n}{n},\frac{2n+1}{3n-4}\right)\in\mathbb{R}^3.
   $$

   1. Guess the limit $$x\in\mathbb{R}^3$$.
   2. Prove that $$x_n\to x$$ using the Euclidean norm.
   3. Prove the general coordinate criterion: a sequence

      $$
      x_n=\bigl(x_n^{(1)},\ldots,x_n^{(m)}\bigr)
      $$

      converges to $$x=\bigl(x^{(1)},\ldots,x^{(m)}\bigr)$$ if and only if $$x_n^{(j)}\to x^{(j)}$$ for every $$j\in\{1,\ldots,m\}$$.

10. **A recursive sequence.** Define $$a_1=1$$ and

    $$
    a_{n+1}=\frac{1}{2}\left(a_n+\frac{3}{a_n}\right)
    \qquad(n\in\mathbb{N}).
    $$

    1. Prove by induction that $$a_n>0$$ for every $$n$$.
    2. Prove that $$a_n\ge\sqrt{3}$$ for every $$n\ge2$$.
    3. Show that $$a_{n+1}\le a_n$$ for every $$n\ge2$$.
    4. Assuming the theorem that every bounded monotone sequence converges, prove that $$(a_n)$$ converges and determine its limit.
    5. Verify the identity

       $$
       a_{n+1}-\sqrt{3}
       =\frac{(a_n-\sqrt{3})^2}{2a_n},
       $$

       and explain what it says about the accuracy of successive approximations to $$\sqrt{3}$$.
