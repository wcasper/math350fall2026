## Complete metric spaces and metric subspaces

### Key words

complete metric space, Cauchy sequence, metric subspace, relative metric, open set in a subspace, completion, equivalence classes of Cauchy sequences, compact implies complete

### Reading assignment

Apostol, Chapter 4: the sections on complete metric spaces, metric subspaces, and completion.

## Practice problems

1. **The same set with two different metrics.** Let $$M=\mathbb{N}$$ and define

   $$
   d_1(m,n)=\lvert m-n\rvert
   $$

   and

   $$
   d_2(m,n)=\left\lvert \frac1m-\frac1n\right\rvert.
   $$

   1. Explain why both $$d_1$$ and $$d_2$$ are metrics on $$M$$.
   2. Prove that every Cauchy sequence in $$(M,d_1)$$ is eventually constant.
   3. Conclude that $$(M,d_1)$$ is complete.
   4. Show that the sequence

      $$
      x_n=n
      $$

      is Cauchy in $$(M,d_2)$$.

   5. Prove that $$(x_n)$$ does not converge to any point of $$(M,d_2)$$.
   6. Conclude that completeness depends on the metric, not merely on the underlying set.

2. **Complete subspaces and closed sets.** Let $$(M,d)$$ be a metric space and let $$S\subseteq M$$ have the relative metric.

   1. Suppose that $$S$$ is complete. Let $$x$$ be an adherent point of $$S$$. For every $$n\in\mathbb{N}$$, choose

      $$
      x_n\in S\cap B\left(x;\frac1n\right).
      $$

      Prove that $$(x_n)$$ is Cauchy in $$S$$.

   2. Use completeness of $$S$$ and uniqueness of limits to prove that $$x\in S$$.
   3. Conclude that every complete metric subspace of $$M$$ is closed in $$M$$.
   4. Now suppose that $$M$$ is complete and that $$S$$ is closed in $$M$$. Prove that $$S$$ is complete.
   5. Conclude that if $$M$$ is complete, then

      $$
      S\text{ is complete}
      \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
      S\text{ is closed in }M.
      $$

   6. Explain why the assumption that $$M$$ is complete is needed for the reverse implication. Find a metric space that is closed in itself but is not complete.

3. **Open sets in a metric subspace.** Let

   $$
   M=\mathbb{R},
   \qquad
   S=[0,2),
   $$

   with their usual metrics.

   1. For $$x\in S$$ and $$r>0$$, prove that

      $$
      B_S(x;r)=B_{\mathbb{R}}(x;r)\cap S.
      $$

   2. Decide whether each of the following sets is open in $$S$$:

      $$
      [0,1),
      \qquad
      (0,2),
      \qquad
      [0,2),
      \qquad
      \{0\}.
      $$

   3. Whenever a set $$U$$ above is open in $$S$$, find an open set $$V\subseteq\mathbb{R}$$ such that

      $$
      U=V\cap S.
      $$

   4. Give an example of a set that is open in $$S$$ but is not open in $$\mathbb{R}$$.
   5. Is $$[0,1]$$ closed in $$S$$? Express it as $$F\cap S$$ for some closed set $$F\subseteq\mathbb{R}$$.

4. **Which subspaces are complete?** Give each set below the metric inherited from $$\mathbb{R}$$. Determine whether it is complete. Justify each answer; for every incomplete space, exhibit a Cauchy sequence that fails to converge in the space.

   1. $$[2,\infty)$$
   2. $$(0,1]$$
   3. $$\mathbb{Z}$$
   4. $$\displaystyle \{0\}\cup\left\{\frac1n:n\in\mathbb{N}\right\}$$
   5. $$\mathbb{Q}\cap[0,1]$$

   You may use the result from Problem 2 together with the completeness of $$\mathbb{R}$$. In part 5, you may use rational truncations of an irrational number in $$(0,1)$$.

5. **Understanding the completion construction.** Let $$(M,d)$$ be a metric space. For Cauchy sequences $$(x_n)$$ and $$(y_n)$$ in $$M$$, define

   $$
   (x_n)\sim(y_n)
   $$

   when

   $$
   d(x_n,y_n)\longrightarrow 0.
   $$

   1. Prove that $$\sim$$ is an equivalence relation on the set of Cauchy sequences in $$M$$.
   2. For $$x\in M$$, let $$c_x=(x,x,x,\ldots)$$ be the constant sequence at $$x$$. Prove that

      $$
      c_x\sim c_y
      $$

      if and only if $$x=y$$.

   3. In the completion, the distance between two equivalence classes is defined by

      $$
      \widehat d([(x_n)],[(y_n)])
      =\lim_{n\to\infty}d(x_n,y_n).
      $$

      Show that

      $$
      \widehat d([c_x],[c_y])=d(x,y).
      $$

      Thus the map $$x\mapsto[c_x]$$ preserves distances.

   4. Suppose that $$M$$ is complete and $$(x_n)$$ is Cauchy. Prove that $$(x_n)$$ is equivalent to a constant sequence.
   5. Explain why a Cauchy sequence of rational numbers converging in $$\mathbb{R}$$ to $$\sqrt2$$ represents a new point in the completion of $$\mathbb{Q}$$.

6. **Completeness versus compactness.** Let $$X$$ be an infinite set equipped with the discrete metric

   $$
   d(x,y)=
   \begin{cases}
   0,&x=y,\\
   1,&x\ne y.
   \end{cases}
   $$

   1. Prove that $$X$$ is complete.
   2. Consider the open cover

      $$
      \left\{B\left(x;\frac12\right):x\in X\right\}.
      $$

      Explain why it has no finite subcover.

   3. Conclude that a discrete metric space is compact if and only if it is finite.
   4. Use this example to show that the converse of

      $$
      \text{compact}\Longrightarrow\text{complete}
      $$

      is false.

   5. Let

      $$
      K=\{0\}\cup\left\{\frac1n:n\in\mathbb{N}\right\}
      \subseteq\mathbb{Q}.
      $$

      Prove directly from the open-cover definition that $$K$$ is compact in $$\mathbb{Q}$$ with the relative Euclidean metric. Conclude that a compact subspace can be complete even when its ambient metric space is not complete.
