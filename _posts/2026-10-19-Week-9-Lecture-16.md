## Practice problems

Throughout, metric subspaces carry the metric inherited from the ambient space.

1. **A two-variable limit that defeats every line test.** Define

   $$
   F(x,y)=\frac{xy^2}{x^2+y^4}
   $$

   for $$(x,y)\ne(0,0)$$.

   1. Fix $$m\in\mathbb{R}$$ and compute

      $$
      \lim_{x\to0}F(x,mx).
      $$

      Also compute the values of $$F$$ along the vertical line $$x=0$$.

   2. Explain why part 1 says that $$F(x,y)\to0$$ along every straight line through the origin.

   3. For

      $$
      p_n=\left(\frac1{n^2},\frac1n\right),
      $$

      prove that $$p_n\to(0,0)$$ and compute $$F(p_n)$$.

   4. For

      $$
      q_n=\left(0,\frac1n\right),
      $$

      prove that $$q_n\to(0,0)$$ and compute $$F(q_n)$$.

   5. Use the sequential criterion to prove that

      $$
      \lim_{(x,y)\to(0,0)}F(x,y)
      $$

      does not exist.

   6. What does this example show about testing a multivariable limit only along straight lines?

2. **Continuity on a discrete metric space.** Let $$S$$ be any set with the discrete metric

   $$
   d_S(x,y)=
   \begin{cases}
   0,&x=y,\\
   1,&x\ne y,
   \end{cases}
   $$

   let $$(T,d_T)$$ be any metric space, and let $$f:S\to T$$ be any function.

   1. Prove that if $$x_n\to p$$ in $$S$$, then $$x_n=p$$ for all sufficiently large $$n$$.

   2. Use the sequential criterion for continuity to prove that $$f$$ is continuous at every point of $$S$$.

   3. Give a direct epsilon-delta proof of the same result. In particular, show that the choice $$\delta=1/2$$ works for every $$\varepsilon>0$$.

   4. If $$A\subseteq S$$, prove that $$A$$ is open in $$S$$. Then use the preimage characterization of continuity to give a third proof that every function $$f:S\to T$$ is continuous.

   5. Explain why the conclusion would be false if the domain were $$\mathbb{R}$$ with its usual metric.

3. **Preimages and relative openness.** Let

   $$
   f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R},
   \qquad
   f(x)=x^2.
   $$

   1. Compute each preimage:

      $$
      f^{-1}((-1,4)),
      \qquad
      f^{-1}([1,9]),
      \qquad
      f^{-1}((4,\infty)).
      $$

   2. Verify directly that the preimages of the open sets in part 1 are open and that the preimage of the closed set is closed.

   3. Let $$A=[0,2]$$ and let $$g=f|_A:A\to\mathbb{R}$$ be the restriction of $$f$$. Compute

      $$
      g^{-1}((-1,1)).
      $$

   4. The set found in part 3 is not open in $$\mathbb{R}$$. Find an open set $$U\subseteq\mathbb{R}$$ such that

      $$
      g^{-1}((-1,1))=U\cap A,
      $$

      and explain why this proves that the preimage is open in the subspace $$A$$.

   5. Explain why these computations are consistent with the continuity of $$f$$ and $$g$$.

4. **Compactness and the extreme value theorem.** Define

   $$
   p(x)=x^2-4x+5=(x-2)^2+1.
   $$

   1. Explain why the extreme value theorem applies to $$p$$ on $$K=[-1,5]$$ before trying to locate any extrema.

   2. Find the absolute minimum and absolute maximum of $$p$$ on $$K$$, including the points where they occur.

   3. Determine the image $$p(K)$$. Explain why your answer is both closed and bounded, as predicted by the theorem that continuous images of compact sets are compact.

   4. Use the intermediate value theorem to explain why every number between the minimum and maximum actually belongs to $$p(K)$$.

   5. Now replace $$K$$ by the open interval $$(-1,5)$$. Determine the infimum and supremum of $$p((-1,5))$$, and state which of them are attained.

   6. Explain precisely which conclusion of the extreme value theorem can fail in part 5 and which hypothesis has been lost.

5. **Recognizing connected and disconnected sets.** Give every set below the metric inherited from $$\mathbb{R}$$:

   $$
   A=(-2,-1]\cup[1,3),
   \qquad
   B=\mathbb{Q},
   \qquad
   C=[0,1].
   $$

   1. Define

      $$
      U=A\cap(-\infty,0)
      \qquad\text{and}\qquad
      V=A\cap(0,\infty).
      $$

      Prove that $$U$$ and $$V$$ are nonempty, disjoint, open in $$A$$, and satisfy $$A=U\cup V$$. Conclude that $$A$$ is disconnected.

   2. Are $$U$$ and $$V$$ open in $$\mathbb{R}$$? Explain why connectedness of $$A$$ uses openness in the subspace $$A$$ rather than openness in the ambient space.

   3. Let

      $$
      U_{\mathbb{Q}}=\mathbb{Q}\cap(-\infty,\sqrt2)
      \qquad\text{and}\qquad
      V_{\mathbb{Q}}=\mathbb{Q}\cap(\sqrt2,\infty).
      $$

      Use these sets to prove that $$\mathbb{Q}$$ is disconnected.

   4. Why is the fact that $$\sqrt2\notin\mathbb{Q}$$ essential in part 3?

   5. Explain why the theorem about intervals implies that $$C$$ is connected.

   6. For each of the following sets, decide whether it is connected and justify your answer:

      $$
      (0,1),
      \qquad
      [0,1)\cup(1,2],
      \qquad
      \{0\},
      \qquad
      \mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}.
      $$

6. **Two applications of the intermediate value theorem.**

   1. Let

      $$
      h(x)=x^3+x-1.
      $$

      Show that $$h$$ has a zero in $$(0,1)$$.

   2. Prove that $$h$$ has at most one zero by showing that $$h(y)>h(x)$$ whenever $$y>x$$. Conclude that the zero in part 1 is unique.

   3. Let $$f:[0,1]\to[0,1]$$ be continuous and define

      $$
      g(x)=f(x)-x.
      $$

      Prove that $$g$$ is continuous, $$g(0)\geq0$$, and $$g(1)\leq0$$.

   4. Use the intermediate value theorem to prove that there exists $$c\in[0,1]$$ such that

      $$
      f(c)=c.
      $$

      Such a point is called a **fixed point** of $$f$$.

   5. Explain how connectedness is hiding behind both applications: a continuous function on an interval cannot pass from one side of a value to the other without taking that value.
