## Practice problems

Throughout, $$(M,d)$$ denotes a metric space.

1. **Different subsequences, different behavior.** Consider the sequence

   $$
   x_n=(-1)^n+\frac1n.
   $$

   1. Write the first six terms of $$(x_n)$$.
   2. Find formulas for the subsequences $$(x_{2k})$$ and $$(x_{2k-1})$$.
   3. Determine the limit of each of these two subsequences.
   4. Explain why the original sequence $$(x_n)$$ does not converge.
   5. Consider the subsequence $$(x_{k^2})$$. Does it converge? Justify your answer by finding two appropriate subsequences of $$(x_{k^2})$$.

2. **A subsequence test for failure of convergence.** Let $$(x_n)$$ be a sequence in $$M$$ and let $$L\in M$$.

   1. Write the negation of the statement $$x_n\to L$$. Your answer should begin with

      $$
      \text{There exists }\varepsilon_0>0\text{ such that}\ldots
      $$

   2. Prove that $$x_n$$ does not converge to $$L$$ if and only if there are some $$\varepsilon_0>0$$ and a subsequence $$(x_{k_j})$$ such that

      $$
      d(x_{k_j},L)\geq \varepsilon_0
      $$

      for every $$j\in\mathbb{N}$$.

   3. In the forward direction, explain carefully how to choose

      $$
      k_1<k_2<k_3<\cdots.
      $$

   4. Use this result to show that if every subsequence of $$(x_n)$$ has a further subsequence converging to $$L$$, then $$x_n\to L$$.

3. **A recursively defined monotone sequence.** Define

   $$
   x_1=1,
   \qquad
   x_{n+1}=\sqrt{2+x_n}.
   $$

   1. Compute the first four terms.
   2. Prove by induction that

      $$
      1\leq x_n<2
      $$

      for every $$n\in\mathbb{N}$$.

   3. Prove that $$(x_n)$$ is increasing. You may find it useful to factor

      $$
      2+x_n-x_n^2.
      $$

   4. Use the monotone convergence theorem to prove that $$(x_n)$$ converges.
   5. Determine its limit.
   6. Prove the error estimate

      $$
      0<2-x_n\leq \frac{1}{3^{n-1}}.
      $$

4. **Testing the Cauchy condition.** For each sequence below, determine whether it is Cauchy. Give a proof directly from the definition.

   1. The sequence

      $$
      a_n=\frac1n.
      $$

   2. The sequence of partial sums

      $$
      b_n=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac1{2^k}.
      $$

   3. The sequence of partial sums

      $$
      c_n=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac1k.
      $$

      For this part, compare $$c_{2n}$$ with $$c_n$$ and show that

      $$
      c_{2n}-c_n\geq \frac12.
      $$

   4. Use the theorem that every convergent sequence is Cauchy to determine which of these sequences cannot converge.

5. **A Cauchy sequence with a convergent subsequence.** Suppose that $$(x_n)$$ is a Cauchy sequence in $$M$$ and that some subsequence satisfies

   $$
   x_{k_j}\longrightarrow L.
   $$

   1. Let $$\varepsilon>0$$. Use the Cauchy property to find $$N_1$$ such that

      $$
      d(x_m,x_n)<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}
      $$

      whenever $$m,n\geq N_1$$.

   2. Use convergence of the subsequence to choose $$j$$ so that

      $$
      k_j\geq N_1
      \qquad\text{and}\qquad
      d(x_{k_j},L)<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.
      $$

   3. Apply the triangle inequality to prove that $$x_n\to L$$.
   4. Deduce that a Cauchy sequence cannot have two convergent subsequences with different limits.
   5. Give a short proof that every Cauchy sequence in $$\mathbb{R}^m$$ converges by combining this result with the boundedness of Cauchy sequences and the Bolzano--Weierstrass theorem.

6. **A missing point in $$\mathbb{Q}$$.** Equip $$\mathbb{Q}$$ with the usual metric

   $$
   d(x,y)=\lvert x-y\rvert.
   $$

   For each $$n\in\mathbb{N}$$, let

   $$
   q_n=\frac{\lfloor 10^n\sqrt{2}\rfloor}{10^n}.
   $$

   Thus, $$q_n$$ is the decimal expansion of $$\sqrt2$$ truncated after $$n$$ decimal places.

   1. Explain why $$q_n\in\mathbb{Q}$$ and prove that

      $$
      0\leq \sqrt2-q_n<10^{-n}.
      $$

   2. Prove directly that $$(q_n)$$ is a Cauchy sequence in $$\mathbb{Q}$$.
   3. Prove that $$(q_n)$$ does not converge to any point of $$\mathbb{Q}$$.
   4. Why does this not contradict the theorem that every Cauchy sequence in $$\mathbb{R}^m$$ converges?
   5. Explain precisely which point is “missing” from the metric space $$\mathbb{Q}$$ in this example.
