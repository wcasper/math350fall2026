## Series tests and infinite products

### Key words

comparison test, limit comparison test, limit inferior, limit superior, ratio test, root test, infinite product, partial product, absolute convergence of a product, Weierstrass product, Euler product, Riemann zeta function

### Reading assignment

Apostol, Chapter 8: the sections on comparison tests, the ratio and root tests, and infinite products.

## Practice problems

1. **Comparison and limit comparison.** Determine whether each series converges or diverges. Justify every comparison.

   1. Prove directly that, for every positive integer $$n$$,

      $$
      0\leq\frac{3n+1}{n^3+4}\leq\frac4{n^2}.
      $$

      Use the comparison test to analyze

      $$
      \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{3n+1}{n^3+4}.
      $$

   2. Verify your answer to part 1 using limit comparison with $$\sum 1/n^2$$.
   3. Use limit comparison with the harmonic series to analyze

      $$
      \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{n^2+1}{n^3+2}.
      $$

   4. Give a direct lower comparison which also proves the conclusion in part 3.
   5. Analyze

      $$
      \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac1{n^2+\sin^2 n}
      $$

      by direct comparison.
   6. Explain why knowing only that $$0\leq a_n\leq b_n$$ is not enough to transfer convergence or divergence in both directions. State the two valid directions of the comparison test.

2. **Computing limit inferior and limit superior.** Recall that

   $$
   \liminf_{n\to\infty}a_n
   =\lim_{n\to\infty}\inf_{k\geq n}a_k
   $$

   and

   $$
   \limsup_{n\to\infty}a_n
   =\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{k\geq n}a_k.
   $$

   1. Let

      $$
      a_n=(-1)^n+\frac1n.
      $$

      Find $$\liminf a_n$$ and $$\limsup a_n$$.
   2. Find two subsequences of $$(a_n)$$ which converge to the two values from part 1.
   3. Define

      $$
      b_n=
      \begin{cases}
      2,&n\text{ is a perfect square},\\
      0,&n\text{ is not a perfect square}.
      \end{cases}
      $$

      Find $$\liminf b_n$$ and $$\limsup b_n$$.
   4. Explain why the ordinary limit of $$(b_n)$$ does not exist even though both its limit inferior and limit superior do.
   5. Let $$(x_n)$$ be a bounded sequence. Prove that

      $$
      \liminf_{n\to\infty}x_n
      \leq
      \limsup_{n\to\infty}x_n.
      $$

   6. Prove that a bounded sequence converges to $$L$$ if and only if

      $$
      \liminf_{n\to\infty}x_n
      =
      \limsup_{n\to\infty}x_n
      =L.
      $$

3. **Ratio and root tests.** For each series, decide which test is most natural, compute the relevant limit or limit superior, and state the conclusion.

   1. Analyze

      $$
      \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{3^n}{n!}.
      $$

   2. Analyze

      $$
      \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{n^2}{2^n}.
      $$

   3. Analyze

      $$
      \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}
      \left(\frac{2+(-1)^n}{4}\right)^n.
      $$

      Notice that the sequence of $$n$$th roots has two different subsequential limits.
   4. Apply the root test to

      $$
      \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac1{n^2}
      $$

      and to

      $$
      \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac1n.
      $$

      What answer does the test give in each case?
   5. Explain why part 4 shows that a ratio or root equal to $$1$$ is genuinely inconclusive.
   6. Construct one convergent geometric series and one divergent geometric series, and verify that the ratio and root tests give the expected conclusions.

4. **A telescoping infinite product—and a trap.** Consider first the product

   $$
   \prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(1+\frac1{2^n}\right).
   $$

   1. Multiply its first three factors and prove that

      $$
      \left(1+\frac12\right)
      \left(1+\frac14\right)
      \left(1+\frac18\right)>2.
      $$

      Explain why this product cannot equal $$2$$.
   2. The familiar identity $$(1-t)(1+t)=1-t^2$$ allows telescoping only if the next factor is $$1+t^2$$. Explain why factors with exponents

      $$
      1,2,4,8,\ldots
      $$

      work, while factors with exponents

      $$
      1,2,3,4,\ldots
      $$

      do not.

   Now fix a real number $$x$$ with $$\lvert x\rvert<1$$ and, for $$N\geq0$$, define

   $$
   P_N=\prod_{k=0}^{N}\left(1+x^{2^k}\right).
   $$

   3. Use induction to prove that

      $$
      (1-x)P_N=1-x^{2^{N+1}}.
      $$

   4. Deduce that

      $$
      P_N=\frac{1-x^{2^{N+1}}}{1-x}.
      $$

   5. Prove that

      $$
      \prod_{k=0}^{\infty}\left(1+x^{2^k}\right)
      =\frac1{1-x}.
      $$

   6. Set $$x=1/2$$ and obtain the corrected telescoping identity

      $$
      \prod_{k=0}^{\infty}\left(1+\frac1{2^{2^k}}\right)=2.
      $$

5. **When does a positive infinite product converge?** Let $$a_n\geq0$$ and define

   $$
   P_N=\prod_{n=1}^{N}(1+a_n).
   $$

   Thus, in this problem, every partial product is at least $$1$$, and convergence means that $$P_N$$ approaches a finite limit.

   1. Use the Mean Value Theorem to prove that, for $$0\leq x\leq1$$,

      $$
      \frac{x}{2}\leq\log(1+x)\leq x.
      $$

   2. Suppose that $$\sum a_n$$ converges. Prove that

      $$
      0\leq\sum_{n=1}^{N}\log(1+a_n)\leq\sum_{n=1}^{N}a_n.
      $$

      Deduce that $$\prod(1+a_n)$$ converges.
   3. Conversely, suppose that $$\prod(1+a_n)$$ converges. Prove that $$a_n\to0$$.
   4. Deduce that $$0\leq a_n\leq1$$ for all sufficiently large $$n$$, and use part 1 to prove that $$\sum a_n$$ converges.
   5. Conclude that, for nonnegative terms,

      $$
      \prod_{n=1}^{\infty}(1+a_n)\text{ converges}
      \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
      \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_n\text{ converges}.
      $$

   6. Use this theorem to determine whether each product converges:

      $$
      \prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(1+\frac1{n^2}\right),
      \qquad
      \prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(1+\frac1n\right).
      $$

6. **Building Euler's product from unique factorization.** Let

   $$
   2=p_1<p_2<p_3<\cdots
   $$

   be the primes, let $$s>1$$, and define

   $$
   \zeta(s)=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac1{m^s}.
   $$

   1. Use the geometric-series formula to prove that

      $$
      \frac1{1-p_j^{-s}}
      =1+\frac1{p_j^s}+\frac1{p_j^{2s}}+\cdots.
      $$

   2. Let $$S_N$$ be the set of positive integers whose prime factors all belong to

      $$
      \{p_1,p_2,\ldots,p_N\}.
      $$

      Use unique factorization to prove that

      $$
      \prod_{j=1}^{N}\frac1{1-p_j^{-s}}
      =\sum_{m\in S_N}\frac1{m^s}.
      $$

   3. Prove that the expressions in part 2 form an increasing sequence bounded above by $$\zeta(s)$$.
   4. Fix $$M\in\mathbb{N}$$. Prove that every integer $$m\leq M$$ belongs to $$S_N$$ for all sufficiently large $$N$$.
   5. Use part 4 to prove Euler's product formula

      $$
      \zeta(s)=\prod_{p\text{ prime}}\frac1{1-p^{-s}}.
      $$

   6. Using the known value $$\zeta(2)=\pi^2/6$$, conclude that

      $$
      \prod_{p\text{ prime}}\frac1{1-p^{-2}}=\frac{\pi^2}{6}.
      $$
