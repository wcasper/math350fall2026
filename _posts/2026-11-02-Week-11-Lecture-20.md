## Practice problems

1. **Critical points and extrema.** In this problem, a critical point of a function is a point at which the derivative is zero or does not exist.

   1. Let

      $$
      f(x)=x^3.
      $$

      Find all critical points of $$f$$. Prove that its critical point is neither a local maximum nor a local minimum.
   2. Let

      $$
      g(x)=\lvert x\rvert.
      $$

      Prove that $$0$$ is a critical point and a global minimum, even though $$g'(0)$$ does not exist.
   3. Let

      $$
      h(x)=x^4-2x^2.
      $$

      Find all critical points of $$h$$ and classify each one as a local maximum, a local minimum, or neither.
   4. Which examples above show that the converse of

      $$
      \text{interior local extremum}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\text{critical point}
      $$

      is false?
   5. Consider $$p(x)=x$$ on the domain $$[0,1]$$. Identify its global maximum and minimum. Explain why this example shows that the word *interior* is necessary in the critical-point theorem.

2. **Why an interior extremum is a critical point.** Let $$f:A\to\mathbb{R}$$, let $$c$$ be an interior point of $$A\subseteq\mathbb{R}$$, and suppose that $$f$$ has a local maximum at $$c$$.

   1. Prove that there is some $$r>0$$ such that

      $$
      f(c+h)-f(c)\leq0
      $$

      whenever $$0<\lvert h\rvert<r$$.
   2. Prove that, for $$0<h<r$$,

      $$
      \frac{f(c+h)-f(c)}h\leq0.
      $$

   3. Prove that, for $$-r<h<0$$,

      $$
      \frac{f(c+h)-f(c)}h\geq0.
      $$

   4. Assume that $$f'(c)$$ exists. Use parts 2 and 3 to prove that $$f'(c)=0$$.
   5. Explain why $$c$$ is still a critical point if $$f'(c)$$ does not exist.
   6. Modify the argument to prove the corresponding result when $$f$$ has a local minimum at $$c$$.

3. **Rolle's theorem and repeated zeros.** Let $$f:[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}$$ be continuous on $$[a,b]$$ and differentiable on $$(a,b)$$.

   1. Assume that $$f(a)=f(b)$$. Use the Extreme Value Theorem to prove Rolle's theorem: there is some $$c\in(a,b)$$ such that $$f'(c)=0$$. Be sure to consider separately the case in which $$f$$ is constant.
   2. Suppose that

      $$
      a\leq a_1<a_2<\cdots<a_m\leq b
      $$

      and that $$f(a_i)=0$$ for every $$i$$. Prove that $$f'$$ has at least $$m-1$$ distinct zeros.
   3. Use part 2 to prove that a nonzero polynomial of degree $$n$$ has at most $$n$$ distinct real roots. You may use induction on $$n$$.
   4. Let $$p$$ be a polynomial of degree at most $$n$$. Suppose that

      $$
      p(a_0)=p(a_1)=\cdots=p(a_n)=0
      $$

      at $$n+1$$ distinct points. Prove that $$p$$ is the zero polynomial.
   5. Deduce that two polynomials of degree at most $$n$$ which agree at $$n+1$$ distinct points must be identical.

4. **Reducing the Mean Value Theorem to Rolle's theorem.** Let $$f:[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}$$ be continuous on $$[a,b]$$ and differentiable on $$(a,b)$$, where $$a<b$$. Define the secant line

   $$
   \ell(x)=f(a)+\frac{f(b)-f(a)}{b-a}(x-a)
   $$

   and let $$g(x)=f(x)-\ell(x)$$.

   1. Prove that $$g$$ is continuous on $$[a,b]$$ and differentiable on $$(a,b)$$.
   2. Prove that $$g(a)=g(b)=0$$.
   3. Apply Rolle's theorem to obtain a point $$c\in(a,b)$$ such that $$g'(c)=0$$.
   4. Compute $$g'(c)$$ and conclude that

      $$
      f'(c)=\frac{f(b)-f(a)}{b-a}.
      $$

   5. Explain geometrically why subtracting $$\ell$$ turns the Mean Value Theorem into Rolle's theorem.
   6. Apply the theorem to $$f(x)=x^2$$ on $$[1,4]$$ and find every point $$c$$ at which the tangent slope equals the secant slope.

5. **Consequences of the Mean Value Theorem.** Let $$I$$ be an interval, and suppose that $$f:I\to\mathbb{R}$$ is differentiable.

   1. Suppose that $$f'(x)=0$$ for every interior point $$x\in I$$. Prove that $$f$$ is constant on $$I$$.
   2. Suppose that $$f'(x)>0$$ for every interior point $$x\in I$$. Prove that $$f$$ is strictly increasing on $$I$$.
   3. State and prove the analogous result when $$f'(x)<0$$.
   4. Suppose that there is a constant $$M\geq0$$ such that

      $$
      \lvert f'(x)\rvert\leq M
      $$

      at every interior point of $$I$$. Prove that, for all $$x,y\in I$$,

      $$
      \lvert f(x)-f(y)\rvert\leq M\lvert x-y\rvert.
      $$

   5. Use part 4 and the fact that $$\lvert\cos t\rvert\leq1$$ to prove that

      $$
      \lvert\sin x-\sin y\rvert\leq\lvert x-y\rvert
      $$

      for all $$x,y\in\mathbb{R}$$.
   6. Set $$y=0$$ to deduce the familiar inequality

      $$
      \lvert\sin x\rvert\leq\lvert x\rvert.
      $$

6. **Three applications of the Mean Value Theorem.** Use the Mean Value Theorem in each part.

   1. A car travels $$150$$ miles in two hours. Prove that at some instant its speed is exactly $$75$$ miles per hour. State the continuity and differentiability assumptions that you need.
   2. Let $$F:[0,1]\to[0,1]$$ be differentiable and suppose that

      $$
      \lvert F'(x)\rvert\leq q<1
      $$

      for every $$x\in(0,1)$$. Prove that $$F$$ has at most one fixed point.
   3. If $$F$$ is also continuous on $$[0,1]$$, apply the Intermediate Value Theorem to $$G(x)=F(x)-x$$ and prove that $$F$$ has a fixed point. Conclude that it has exactly one.
   4. Let $$p$$ be this fixed point. Prove the error estimate

      $$
      \lvert x-p\rvert
      \leq
      \frac{\lvert x-F(x)\rvert}{1-q}
      $$

      for every $$x\in[0,1]$$.
   5. Suppose that $$q=1/4$$ and a computation gives $$\lvert x-F(x)\rvert<10^{-6}$$. Give a certified bound for $$\lvert x-p\rvert$$.
   6. Explain why a small residual $$\lvert x-F(x)\rvert$$ is useful only when the factor $$1/(1-q)$$ is controlled.
