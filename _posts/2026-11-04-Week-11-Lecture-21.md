## Practice problems

1. **A series is encoded by its partial sums.** Let

   $$
   s_n=a_1+a_2+\cdots+a_n
   $$

   be the sequence of partial sums of a series.

   1. Prove that

      $$
      a_1=s_1
      $$

      and, for every $$n\geq2$$,

      $$
      a_n=s_n-s_{n-1}.
      $$

   2. Suppose that

      $$
      s_n=2-\frac1n.
      $$

      Find $$a_1$$ and a formula for $$a_n$$ when $$n\geq2$$.
   3. Determine whether the series in part 2 converges. If it converges, find its sum.
   4. Suppose instead that

      $$
      s_n=(-1)^n+\frac1n.
      $$

      Does the corresponding series converge? Explain your answer directly from the definition.
   5. Suppose that the series

      $$
      \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_n
      $$

      converges, and define a new sequence $$(b_n)$$ by changing only finitely many of the terms $$a_n$$. Prove that

      $$
      \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}b_n
      $$

      also converges.
   6. How does changing finitely many terms affect the sum of a convergent series?

2. **A family of telescoping series.** Fix a real number $$\alpha\geq0$$ and consider

   $$
   \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}
   \frac1{(n+\alpha)(n+\alpha+1)}.
   $$

   1. Prove the identity

      $$
      \frac1{(n+\alpha)(n+\alpha+1)}
      =\frac1{n+\alpha}-\frac1{n+\alpha+1}.
      $$

   2. Write out the first four terms of the $$N$$th partial sum and indicate which terms cancel.
   3. Prove that

      $$
      s_N=\frac1{1+\alpha}-\frac1{N+\alpha+1}.
      $$

   4. Find the sum of the series.
   5. Let $$s$$ denote the sum. Prove the exact remainder formula

      $$
      \lvert s-s_N\rvert=\frac1{N+\alpha+1}.
      $$

   6. Given $$\varepsilon>0$$, find a condition on $$N$$ that guarantees

      $$
      \lvert s-s_N\rvert<\varepsilon.
      $$

3. **Geometric series and repeating decimals.** Let $$r\in\mathbb{R}$$ and define

   $$
   s_N=r+r^2+\cdots+r^N.
   $$

   1. Assume $$r\ne1$$. Multiply $$s_N$$ by $$1-r$$ and prove that

      $$
      s_N=\frac{r-r^{N+1}}{1-r}.
      $$

   2. Prove that if $$\lvert r\rvert<1$$, then

      $$
      \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}r^n=\frac{r}{1-r}.
      $$

   3. Analyze separately the cases $$r=1$$, $$r=-1$$, and $$\lvert r\rvert>1$$. For which real values of $$r$$ does the series converge?
   4. Write the repeating decimal

      $$
      0.272727\ldots
      $$

      as a geometric series and use the formula from part 2 to express it as a fraction.
   5. More generally, let $$m$$ be an integer with $$0\leq m\leq99$$. Prove that

      $$
      0.\overline{m}=\frac{m}{99},
      $$

      where $$0.\overline{m}$$ denotes the decimal obtained by repeating the two-digit block $$m$$ forever. Interpret a one-digit value of $$m$$ as having a leading zero.

4. **The Cauchy condition detects harmonic divergence.** Consider the harmonic series

   $$
   \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac1n.
   $$

   1. Suppose that

      $$
      \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_n
      $$

      converges. Use

      $$
      a_n=s_n-s_{n-1}
      $$

      to prove that $$a_n\to0$$. This proves the divergence test from the lecture.
   2. Explain why the divergence test does not decide whether the harmonic series converges.
   3. For every positive integer $$N$$, prove that

      $$
      \frac1{N+1}+\frac1{N+2}+\cdots+\frac1{2N}
      \geq\frac12.
      $$

   4. Use part 3 and the Cauchy condition to prove that the harmonic series diverges.
   5. Explain why this example proves that the condition $$a_n\to0$$ is necessary but not sufficient for the convergence of

      $$
      \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_n.
      $$

   6. Contrast the harmonic series with

      $$
      \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac1{n(n+1)}.
      $$

      Use telescoping to prove directly that the second series satisfies the Cauchy condition.

5. **Alternating series and certified error.** Consider

   $$
   s=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n+1}}n.
   $$

   1. Verify the hypotheses of the alternating series test and conclude that the series converges.
   2. Let $$s_N$$ be the $$N$$th partial sum. Prove that

      $$
      \lvert s-s_N\rvert<\frac1{N+1}.
      $$

   3. Find the smallest integer $$N$$ for which the estimate in part 2 guarantees

      $$
      \lvert s-s_N\rvert<10^{-3}.
      $$

   4. Group consecutive terms to show that

      $$
      s_2<s_4<s_6<\cdots<s<\cdots<s_5<s_3<s_1.
      $$

   5. Give a certified interval containing $$s=\ln 2$$ whose endpoints are $$s_{10}$$ and $$s_{11}$$.
   6. Show that

      $$
      \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n+1}}{\sqrt n}
      $$

      converges.
   7. Adapt the block argument from Problem 4 to prove that

      $$
      \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac1{\sqrt n}
      $$

      diverges. Conclude that the series in part 6 is conditionally convergent.

6. **Why absolute convergence implies convergence.** Suppose that

   $$
   \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lvert a_n\rvert
   $$

   converges.

   1. Use the Cauchy condition to prove that for every $$\varepsilon>0$$ there exists $$N$$ such that, whenever $$n\geq N$$ and $$p\in\mathbb{Z}^+$$,

      $$
      \lvert a_n\rvert+\lvert a_{n+1}\rvert+\cdots+\lvert a_{n+p}\rvert
      <\varepsilon.
      $$

   2. Use the triangle inequality to prove that

      $$
      \lvert a_n+a_{n+1}+\cdots+a_{n+p}\rvert
      <\varepsilon.
      $$

   3. Apply the Cauchy condition again to prove the absolute convergence theorem:

      $$
      \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lvert a_n\rvert\text{ converges}
      \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
      \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_n\text{ converges}.
      $$

   4. For $$n\geq2$$, prove that

      $$
      \frac1{n^2}\leq\frac1{n(n-1)}
      =\frac1{n-1}-\frac1n.
      $$

   5. Use the Cauchy condition and the telescoping expression in part 4 to prove that

      $$
      \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac1{n^2}
      $$

      converges.
   6. Conclude that

      $$
      \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^n}{n^2}
      $$

      converges absolutely.
   7. Explain the difference between the convergence in part 6 and the conditional convergence of the alternating harmonic series.
